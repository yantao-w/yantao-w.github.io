Dim Sum 101 (点心): A Beginner's Guide to Cantonese Bites

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Dim sum — the Cantonese tradition of small, steamed and fried bites served with tea — is one of the world's great food experiences. Whether you're ordering at a restaurant or trying to make it at home, here's your guide to this beloved brunch ritual.

What Is Dim Sum?

Dim sum means "touch the heart" — a collection of small dishes served in bamboo steamers with tea, traditionally on weekend mornings. You share, you graze, and you try a little bit of everything.

Must-Try Classics

  • Har gow (shrimp dumplings) — translucent wrappers around plump shrimp
  • Siu mai (pork dumplings) — open-topped, juicy, and savory
  • Char siu bao (BBQ pork buns) — soft, fluffy, sweet-savory
  • Cheung fun (rice noodle rolls) — silky rolls with shrimp or beef
  • Egg tarts — flaky pastry with a smooth custard center

How to Order

Start with a few steamed classics, then add fried and baked items. Share everything, order in rounds as you go, and pair it all with hot tea — jasmine or oolong are traditional.

Can You Make It at Home?

Yes, but start easy. Frozen dim sum from an Asian grocery steams well at home, and homemade dumplings are a natural place to begin. For har gow and siu mai, ready-made wrappers make it approachable.

The Dim Sum Experience

The beauty of dim sum is variety and sharing. Go with friends, order widely, and let the table fill with steamers. It's a celebration — and the perfect weekend ritual.

Love these flavors? Try chow mein or sweet and sour pork.
