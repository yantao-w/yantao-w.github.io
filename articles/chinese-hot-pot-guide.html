Chinese Hot Pot at Home (火锅): A Complete Beginner's Guide

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Hot pot is one of the most loved, most social ways to eat — and it's taken the world by storm on social media. The good news: you can absolutely do it at home with simple ingredients. Here's your complete beginner's guide.

What Is Hot Pot?

A bubbling pot of broth sits in the center of the table. You cook thinly sliced meat, vegetables, tofu, and noodles in the broth yourself, then dip each bite in a custom sauce. It's a 2-3 hour social meal — fun, interactive, and endlessly customizable.

The Broth

Start simple. Use a good store-bought or homemade broth, then add aromatics: ginger, garlic, scallions, and dried chili for a Sichuan-style spicy base, or keep it mild with a clear chicken or mushroom broth. For the classic fiery Sichuan flavor, add Sichuan peppercorns for that numbing tingle.

What to Cook

  • Thinly sliced beef, lamb, or chicken — must be paper-thin so it cooks in seconds
  • Tofu and tofu skin — soak up all the flavor
  • Mushrooms, napa cabbage, lotus root — great textures
  • Fish balls, shrimp, and dumplings — hot pot favorites
  • Noodles or rice — to finish the meal in the flavorful broth

Dipping Sauces

Set out a sauce bar: soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic, chili oil, scallions, and a splash of vinegar. Everyone mixes their own. The classic Sichuan combo is a sesame-oil-and-garlic dip — cooling against the spice.

How to Host

Set up a portable stove or hot pot pot, keep the broth simmering, and serve everything raw on plates around the table. Cook small batches, chat, and dip. It's the easiest impressive dinner you'll ever host.

Craving more classics? Try mapo tofu or homemade dumplings.
